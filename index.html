Reading the whole story

This is the last lesson of Chapter 1, and there is no new grammar. You use everything you have learned to read a story. The story is in two parts, and before each part there is a short practice section.

Last lesson the same four mistakes kept coming up, so today’s practice is about them:

  • reading a word ending -am, -um, -as, -os as the subject, when it comes first in the sentence;
  • putting an -as or -os word after a part of sum;
  • calling the noun after a preposition the object;
  • forgetting one or many after a preposition: per hortos is through the gardens.

2 · Who is doing it?

Latin can put any word first. Last lesson, when a sentence began with an accusative, many of you made that word the subject. epistulam ad dominum portat came out as “the letter is carried to the master” or “the master carries the letter”. It means “he carries the letter to the master”.

Rule 1. The subject is always nominative. Look for -a or -us (one), -ae or -i (more than one). A word ending -am, -um, -as or -os is never the subject, wherever it stands. In servos dominus vocat, dominus is the subject: the master calls the slaves.

Rule 2. No nominative? The subject is inside the verb. The ending tells you which person: -t he, she or it; -mus we; -tis you; -nt they. And portat always means “he carries”. It never means “is carried”: someone is doing the carrying.

Rule 3. In a story, the subject stays the same until a new one is named. Your textbook gives this rule for joined-up Latin. nuntius ad villam ambulat. epistulam portat. The second sentence has no nominative, so the messenger is still the subject: “The messenger walks to the house. He carries a letter.”

3 · Story part 1: on the island

The story so far. A messenger brought the master a letter, and the household got ready to sail to Rome. Davus and Syrus took the money but did not go to the horses. A slave-girl ran to the master: “Davus and Syrus are not friends; they are carrying the money to the island!” The master shouted and called his slaves against them.

A slave who ran away with his master’s money was in serious trouble: masters could hire slave-catchers, and a runaway who was caught could be beaten, branded or sold. Read part 1 aloud once, then work through it one sentence at a time.

The Beaz Method: the steps you will follow for each sentence

4 · Three slots

Last lesson sixteen of you got feminae ______ sunt (“the women are goddesses”) wrong: deas, dea and deam all appeared, and only deae is right. After a week of -as and -os following prepositions, the accusative crept into places where it does not belong. Before you choose an ending, decide which slot the noun is in.

SlotCaseExample
the object of a doing verbaccusative: -am, -um, -as, -ospuella deas vocat. The girl calls the goddesses.
after a prepositionaccusative, but not the objectpuella ad deas ambulat. The girl walks towards the goddesses.
with a part of sumnominative: -a, -us, -ae, -i, matching the subject in numberfeminae deae sunt. The women are goddesses.

A part of sum never has an object, and it is never followed by -am, -um, -as or -os. Use the = test: the women = goddesses, so both words are nominative.
